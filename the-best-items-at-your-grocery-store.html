The Absolute Best Items to Buy at Your Grocery Store Right Now

From the produce aisle to the freezer section, here's what's worth putting in your cart this season — and why quality matters more than you think.

By Caroline Muir  ·  Published March 12, 2026  ·  8 min read

There's a particular kind of satisfaction that comes from a well-executed grocery run. Not the frantic, forgot-the-list, grab-whatever-is-closest kind of trip, but the deliberate sort — the one where you come home with a bag full of ingredients that genuinely make you excited to cook. After years of testing products, talking to chefs, and spending more time in grocery aisles than I'd care to admit, I've developed some strong opinions about what's actually worth buying. This is a guide to the items that consistently deliver, aisle by aisle.

The Produce Section Is Where It All Starts

If there is one area of the grocery store where quality variance is enormous, it's the produce section. The difference between a mealy, flavorless tomato and a vine-ripe heirloom is the difference between a forgettable dinner and a memorable one. This time of year, I'd steer you toward avocados first and foremost. A ripe Hass avocado is one of the most versatile ingredients in your kitchen — mashed onto toast with flaky salt, sliced into grain bowls, blended into smoothies for creaminess, or turned into a quick guacamole for an impromptu gathering. When they're good, they're transcendent. The trick is buying them slightly firm and letting them ripen on your counter for a day or two.

Fresh berries are another produce-aisle highlight right now. Strawberries are coming into their peak season, and when you find ones that actually smell like strawberries from a foot away, buy as many as you can carry. They're perfect for snacking, tossing into yogurt, or baking into a rustic galette. Blueberries, meanwhile, are nutritional powerhouses that freeze beautifully if you overbuy — just spread them on a sheet pan, freeze for an hour, and transfer to a bag. You'll be grateful in July when you're making smoothies.

Don't overlook leafy greens, especially the bagged salad mixes and fresh kale. A bag of pre-washed arugula or spring mix is the kind of shortcut that actually encourages you to eat more salads, because the barrier to entry is essentially zero. Kale, whether curly or lacinato, has become a weeknight staple for good reason: it holds up in soups, gets crispy when roasted with olive oil, and makes a hearty base for grain bowls that doesn't wilt the second you add a warm protein on top.

The Meat and Seafood Counter Deserves Your Attention

I'll be direct: the quality gap between good chicken breast and mediocre chicken breast is worth the extra dollar per pound. Look for air-chilled chicken if your store carries it — the texture is noticeably better because the meat hasn't absorbed excess water during processing. This translates to better browning in the pan and more concentrated flavor. Chicken thighs, whether bone-in or boneless and skinless, remain the single best value in the entire protein case. They're nearly impossible to overcook, they take to marinades beautifully, and they cost a fraction of what breast meat does.

For red meat, ground beef is the weeknight workhorse that deserves more respect than it gets. A package of 80/20 ground beef is the starting point for tacos, bolognese, smash burgers, meatballs, chili, and a dozen other meals that can come together in thirty minutes or less. If you're buying steaks, ribeye remains the cut that delivers the most flavor for home cooks who don't want to fuss with technique — the marbling does the work for you.

On the seafood side, salmon fillets are having a moment, and for good reason. A skin-on salmon fillet is one of the fastest, most forgiving proteins to cook at home. Twelve minutes in a hot oven, some salt and pepper, maybe a squeeze of lemon, and you have a dinner that looks and tastes like you spent far more effort than you did. Frozen shrimp is another aisle hero — a bag of peeled, deveined shrimp in the freezer is an emergency dinner waiting to happen. Thaw them under cold running water, sauté with garlic and butter, and toss with pasta or pile onto rice.

The Dairy Aisle Is Quietly Essential

Eggs are the single most useful item in the entire grocery store, and I will defend that position vigorously. A dozen fresh eggs opens up breakfast, lunch, dinner, and baking in ways that no other single ingredient can match. Scrambled, fried, poached, hard-boiled for meal prep, whisked into fried rice, baked into frittatas — the versatility is unmatched at any price point. Buy the best quality you can comfortably afford, whether that's conventional, cage-free, or pasture-raised.

Greek yogurt has earned its permanent spot in the fridge. A tub of plain, full-fat Greek yogurt serves as a high-protein breakfast base when topped with granola and honey, a substitute for sour cream in tacos and baked potatoes, a marinade tenderizer for chicken, and a snack all on its own. The protein content is genuinely impressive — most brands deliver 15 to 20 grams per serving, which makes it one of the most nutrient-dense items in the store.

"The best grocery shopping isn't about finding exotic ingredients. It's about consistently buying the staples that make everyday cooking easier, faster, and more delicious."

Good butter is another dairy-aisle non-negotiable. The difference between a generic store-brand butter and a quality European-style butter with higher fat content is immediately noticeable in baking, on toast, and in pan sauces. A quality block of sharp cheddar or a ball of fresh mozzarella can turn a simple meal into something that feels more intentional. And don't sleep on the shredded cheese section for weeknight convenience — a bag of pre-shredded Mexican blend is the fastest path to quesadillas, sheet-pan nachos, and emergency tacos.

Pantry Staples That Actually Matter

The pantry is where smart grocery shopping pays compound interest. A well-stocked shelf of basics means you're always twenty minutes away from a real meal, even when the fridge looks bare. Dried pasta is the obvious starting point — a box of spaghetti, a box of penne, and maybe a bag of egg noodles covers an enormous range of quick dinners. Pair that with a couple jars of quality pasta sauce (Rao's has become the gold standard, but plenty of store brands have stepped up), and you have the foundation for at least a week's worth of dinners.

Canned goods deserve more love than they typically get. Canned diced tomatoes are arguably the most versatile shelf-stable ingredient in existence — they go into chili, soups, braises, shakshuka, and any number of simmered sauces. Canned beans (black, chickpea, kidney) add protein and substance to almost anything. And a good chicken broth or bone broth is the kind of ingredient that quietly elevates everything it touches, from risotto to reheated leftovers. Stock your pantry with olive oil, a few basic spices — garlic powder, cumin, paprika, Italian seasoning — and you've covered the flavor base for most weeknight cooking.

Rice rounds out the pantry essentials. A bag of jasmine rice or basmati rice is a meal stretcher that pairs with virtually any cuisine, and it stores practically forever. For those pressed on time, the microwave rice pouches have gotten genuinely good and shave fifteen minutes off dinner prep when you need them most.

The Frozen Aisle Is No Longer an Afterthought

There was a time when the frozen section of the grocery store was a wasteland of TV dinners and freezer-burned mystery meat. That era is definitively over. Frozen vegetables are nutritionally comparable to fresh — and in some cases superior, because they're flash-frozen at peak ripeness instead of sitting on a truck for days. A bag of frozen broccoli florets, frozen stir-fry vegetables, or frozen spinach is the kind of kitchen insurance that means you always have something green to throw into a meal.

Frozen fruit has become indispensable for smoothie lovers. A bag of frozen mango chunks, frozen mixed berries, or frozen banana slices means you can blend a breakfast smoothie in sixty seconds without worrying about fresh fruit going bad before you use it. Ice cream, of course, needs no advocacy — but it's worth noting that the frozen aisle has expanded to include everything from açaí bowls to cauliflower-crust pizzas, many of which are genuinely worth keeping in rotation.

Frozen pizza, specifically, has undergone a quiet revolution. The best frozen pizzas today rival what you'd get from a mid-range delivery spot, at a fraction of the cost. Keep one or two in the freezer and you'll never panic about what to feed unexpected guests or hungry kids on a Friday night.

The Snack Aisle and Beverage Section

A good grocery haul isn't complete without the things that make daily life a little more enjoyable. In the snack aisle, the move is to buy what you'll actually eat consistently, not what sounds virtuous. A bag of quality tortilla chips paired with a jar of salsa from the refrigerated section is one of the best snack combinations in the store. Nuts — almonds, cashews, mixed nuts — deliver protein and satisfaction in a way that crackers and pretzels simply can't, and they keep well in the pantry for weeks.

For beverages, keep it simple. Good ground coffee or a box of coffee pods ensures your morning routine doesn't depend on a drive-through. A case of sparkling water or seltzer gives you the fizzy refreshment of soda without the sugar. And if you're a tea drinker, stock a variety — green tea for the afternoons, chamomile for the evenings, and a sturdy black tea for mornings when you want something different from coffee.

The best grocery shopping isn't really about any single item. It's about building a collection of reliable ingredients that you know how to use, that you're excited to eat, and that make the gap between opening the fridge and sitting down to a good meal as short as possible. The items above are the ones that have earned their permanent place in my cart. Start with what resonates, and build from there.
