The Best Gifts for the People Who Are Impossible to Shop For

Every family has one. The relative who already owns everything, returns half of what you buy, and insists they don't need anything. Here's how to get it right anyway.

By Dana Whitfield  ·  Published June 18, 2026  ·  4 min read

We all know the type. You ask what they want and they say "oh, nothing, really." You buy them a sweater and it shows up at the return desk a week later. You try to be thoughtful and end up overthinking it into a candle they'll never light. After enough years of missing, I gave up on guessing what these people own and started thinking about what they actually do. That shift changed everything. The best gift for a hard-to-shop-for person is almost never an object. It's a night out, an afternoon to themselves, a small permission to enjoy something they love.

That's also why gift cards have quietly become the smartest move on this list, not the lazy one. The trick is to skip the generic plastic and pick a card tied to a specific thing the person genuinely enjoys. A card to a place they already love reads as "I know you" rather than "I ran out of time." Below are the two categories that have never once let me down.

For the Movie Lover

Some people light up the moment the lights go down. They have opinions about directors, they read the credits, they will drive across town for a film that's only playing on one screen. For this person, nothing beats the actual experience of going to the theater. The big screen, the sound, the popcorn, the two hours where the phone stays in the pocket and the only thing happening is the movie.

This is the easiest win in gift-giving. A movie theater gift card lets them pick the film, pick the night, and bring whoever they want. Send a gift card to AMC and you've handed them a standing invitation to do the thing they'd choose to do anyway, popcorn included. It works for a teenager and a grandparent equally well, which is rare. Pair it with a note that says "go see something on the big screen, on me," and you've turned a card into a small event.

For the Book Lover

The reader in your life is somehow both the easiest and the hardest person to shop for. Easy because they always want books. Hard because you have no idea which ones they've already read, and buying the wrong novel is worse than buying nothing. The solution is to give them the bookstore itself.

A bookstore gift card hands the choosing back to the one person who knows their taste. Buy a gift card to Barnes and Noble and you've given them an hour of wandering the aisles, a stack of paperbacks, maybe a journal and a fancy coffee while they browse. Readers love the act of choosing almost as much as the reading. You're not guessing at the title. You're funding the afternoon. For anyone who treats a bookstore as a destination rather than an errand, it's close to a perfect gift.

"The best gift for someone who has everything isn't a thing at all. It's an excuse to do more of what they already love."

How to Make It Feel Personal

The only real risk with a gift card is that it feels generic, and that's entirely fixable. Spend the two minutes on presentation. Slip it into a real card with a handwritten line. Tie the gift to a memory: "for the next great movie night," or "for the next stack of books you'll pretend you didn't buy." If you're feeling ambitious, pair two cards into a theme, dinner and a movie, or a bookstore run and a coffee shop afternoon. The card covers the cost. The note covers the thought.

Hard-to-shop-for people aren't actually hard to shop for. They're just particular, and they'd rather choose for themselves than receive one more thing to find space for. Give them the experience and let them fill in the details. You'll stop dreading the question of what to get them, and they'll finally stop returning your gifts.
